TrigScript sounds, step 4: Korobeiniki, steered by the game
===========================================================

(Second build, 18:03: the bass now as loud in the game as it was mixed, and the Space crash turned down with the
music so it can never push it to clipping. The first build's recording went within 0.2% of full scale once.)

Host it as a multiplayer game, alone, with the game's music OFF. Headphones help. Record it.

This is step 3's B cut into 160 bars (1.68 s each) in four layers: drums, bass, harmony (pad, arpeggio, piano),
melody. The game plays one bar of each layer at every bar line, as the program says. Nothing is one long song any
more: each bar is its own sound, starting on its frame, and the bars overlap so nothing is cut off.

1. FIRST, let it play once all the way through WITHOUT touching anything (about 70 s, until "intro" shows again).
   I will compare that pass against the song bar by bar, to measure how evenly the game starts the bars.

2. THEN play with it:
     1 2 3 4 5 6 7   jump to a section at the next bar line: intro, verse, build, drop, break, last verse, outro
     D B H M         drums, bass, harmony, melody: off / on from the next bar line
     L               loop: a section plays again when it ends (press again: on through the song)
     Space           a crash on the next bar line
   Try: loop the verse (2, L), drop out the drums and bring them back, jump to the drop from anywhere (4),
   hit Space a few times, jump to the break mid-phrase (5), and let the outro end it (7, L off).

What to listen for:
1. The first pass: does it sound like step 3's B, or can you hear the bars — a stutter, a flam, a click, a gap? ............
2. Jumps: does a jump land cleanly on the bar line? Does the old section's tail ring into the new one nicely? ............
3. Layers on and off: clean? Does the mix still sound full with one off? ............
4. How quick it feels: a jump waits for the next bar line (up to 1.7 s). Too slow for a game? ............
5. It is about 4 dB quieter than B was: the game adds the bars up itself, with no limiter, so they are turned down
   to leave room. Too quiet? ............
